## Scientific Questions Being Studied
This project investigates how genetic variation and lifestyle factors jointly influence the risk of
six chronic conditions (asthma, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, and colorectal, prostate, and
breast cancer) among African American and Black populations. We ask two questions. First, which
genetic variants and lifestyle factors are associated with each condition in a large national
African American cohort? Second, do these genetic and health patterns differ between the national
All of Us cohort and a local community cohort from Hillsborough, North Carolina? Because African
Americans remain underrepresented in genomic research, characterizing these associations is
important for understanding disease risk and informing prevention in this population.

## Scientific Approaches
We define the six phenotypes from a shared, version-controlled codebook of OMOP condition concept
sets and case rules, applied identically to both cohorts so comparisons stay consistent. Using the
All of Us Controlled Tier data, we build an African American cohort, extract phenotypes and
demographic covariates, and pull genomic data (microarray and exome PLINK files). We then run
quality control and association or risk analyses, primarily logistic regression adjusted for age,
sex, and lifestyle factors (smoking, alcohol, physical activity, and sleep), to identify genetic and
lifestyle correlates of each condition. We also compare prevalence and risk between the national
cohort and the Hillsborough community cohort. Analyses are performed in the workbench using Python
and R.

## Anticipated Findings
We anticipate identifying genetic variants and lifestyle factors that are statistically associated
with one or more of the six chronic conditions in African American populations, and characterizing
how prevalence and risk compare between a national cohort and a local community cohort. These
findings will help address the underrepresentation of African Americans in genomic research, improve
understanding of how genes and environment jointly contribute to chronic disease in this population,
and inform prevention and health equity efforts. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed
publications and scientific presentations.
